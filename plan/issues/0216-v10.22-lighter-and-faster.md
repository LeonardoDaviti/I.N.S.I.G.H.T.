# 0216: v10.22, lighter and faster: the plan (Stealth v10.22)

Status: **spec** (branch `v1022-plan` from `v1021` 97d208bf). Research only, nothing built. Read from the code on
`v1021`, and on `v1021-db` (0215's v10.21 part, not yet merged), and from the server by read-only GETs on 2026-11-16
(`/api/status`, `/api/markx/overview`, `/api/operations/background`, `/api/operations/jobs/{id}`; the server runs
v10.20, 85948b7a). Two scratch clones (`prep_mxreal`, `prep_scale`) were read for column sizes, then dropped.

The owner approved eight optimizations on one condition: each is a deepening of the module that owns the decision,
never a parallel path, with no added memory. This plan checks each against the code first. Three of them are already
built or cannot be built as asked; they are rewritten or dropped below, with the reason.

Vocabulary (codebase-design): a **module** has an **interface** (everything a caller must know) at a **seam**; it is
**deep** when a lot of behaviour sits behind a small interface; the **deletion test** asks whether deleting a module
removes complexity (a pass-through) or spreads it over its callers (it earns its keep).

## Item by item

### 1. Answered worker questions drop their input text

**The modules today.**
- `mx_tasks.input text NOT NULL` with `CHECK (input_hash = sha256(input))` and `UNIQUE (kind, guide,
  input_hash)` (`db/migrations/0068_markx_shadow.sql:44-70`).
- One writer: `markx/repo.py:93-114` `add_task`, `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, deduped by the input's hash.
- One place a task becomes answered: `repo.answer_task` (`repo.py:235-239`).
- The input is ~1.5 kB a row (0120's note). Embed tasks have no `mx_calls` row (`embed.py:43`).

**Who reads an answered task's input** (each one checked in the code):
- **Re-asked tasks.** The task is opened again and leased with its input: `decide.Ask.reopen` (`decide.py:719-735`)
  for join, pair, merge, check and perspective; collide (`identity.py:2590`); `graph.after_answer`
  (`graph.py:617`); `back.after_answer` (`back.py:446`).
- **Read after the answer:**
  - `identity.Asked.prime_collide` (`identity.py:2099`, on every decision run);
  - `source_stats.stances` (`source_stats.py:439, 790, 813`);
  - `track_subtopics.apply_names` / `apply_place` (`track_subtopics.py:588, 778`);
  - `graph.verdicts` `linked(input)` (`graph.py:587, 639`);
  - `rename.py:399-416` (the "asked because" evidence);
  - `labels.item_view` (`labels.py:593`; gone with item 6).
- **Short inputs only:** `embed.set_aside` reads `length(input) <= SCAN_CHARS` (400) (`embed.py:229`).
- **The real-data invariant** `_markx_calls_valid` validates every valid call against its input again
  (`checks/invariants.py:424`).
- **The hash alone, never the text:** `incremental.py:258`, `tasks.reasked` (`tasks.py:873`), `finders.py:163`,
  `tracks.py:265`, `embed.py:248, 417`, the dedupe itself.

**The friction.** About 90% of the rows are kinds whose input is never read once they are answered: entities
343k, alias 357k, embed_name 336k, read 155k, embed 155k, track 128k, cite 42k (the overview's queue). Their text
is kept for ever, as big as the posts it came from. Not every input can be rebuilt: collide, stance, subtopics,
relate and rename inputs are snapshots of a state at asking time. So the kinds are not all alike, and the decision
belongs to one place.

**The deepened module: `markx/tasks.py` owns a task's input lifetime.**
- **One per-kind fact.** `KEEPS_INPUT` = the kinds above that are re-asked or read after the answer, next to the
  render functions that make the inputs (`tasks.py:375-548`). Every other kind's input is **spent**.
- **One function, `tasks.spend(cur, now, *, older=SPEND_AFTER, batch=...)`.**
  - It sets `input = NULL` on answered tasks of spent kinds answered more than `SPEND_AFTER` ago (14 days).
  - It leaves alone embed inputs of `SCAN_CHARS` or less, so `set_aside` stays exact.
  - It runs in batches of ~20k, in the scheduler's daily work (`run_scheduler.py:208-221`, beside
    `prune_job_runs`), one transaction a batch.
  - Why 14 days and not at the answer: the invariant, the worker's same-cycle re-asks (0214) and debugging keep a
    window of recent inputs.
  - Why not inside `answer_task`: that would put two decisions in one statement.
- **The seam stays the table.** `input_hash` stays, so dedupe, `mx_inc_inputs` and every hash reader are
  unchanged. A planner rendering the same question again still finds the answered task by its hash and never asks
  it again.
- **A guard test.** Every kind passed to `ask_again`, reopened in SQL, or whose `input` is selected outside the
  lease must be in `KEEPS_INPUT`. It is a source scan, in the way `NoPageStartsWork` works (0206). A future reader
  then cannot silently get NULL.
- **The invariant.** `_markx_calls_valid` validates the calls whose task still has its input: every kept kind,
  plus the last 14 days of the others. That is stated, not hidden.
- **Deletion test.** The input of a spent kind after its answer: delete it and nothing reappears in any caller.
  That is the definition of the column being dead weight.

**Migration (0147).** It contains only these two changes; the daily work does the backfill:
- `input` becomes nullable;
- the CHECK becomes `input IS NULL OR input_hash = sha256(input)`.

The `mx_tasks_counted_*` triggers (0132) fire on the UPDATE. The slice confirms they write no delta rows when only
`input` changes (a `WHEN` clause if not).

**Space back.**
- A plain VACUUM only makes the space reusable inside the table. At ~1,000 new posts a day the table then stops
  growing for months.
- Returning it to the OS needs one `VACUUM FULL mx_tasks`. It is a step for the owner in `DEPLOYMENT.md`: minutes,
  an exclusive lock (the worker's leases wait), and free disk the size of the new table (~1.2 GB). It is never run
  in a migration (`docs/DB.md:13`).

**Expected gain.** About 2.0-2.6 GB of 3.8 GB.
- Basis: rows of the spent kinds × their mean input. On `prep_mxreal` that mean is 0.86-1.15 kB for embed, entities
  and read.
- Measure before: `sum(pg_column_size(input))` by kind on the next server snapshot.
- Measure after: `table_sizes` once the VACUUM FULL has run.
- Must not change: every decision. The slice runs a decision run on a `prep_mxreal` clone before and after
  `spend()`, with 0204's row-digest method; every mx_* table must be identical, `mx_tasks.input` aside.

**Risks.**
- A reader the guard missed. The test covers this, and the 14-day window delays any harm.
- A guide change that needs old questions asked again makes new tasks (a new guide means a new hash). Nothing is
  rebuilt from a NULL.

### 2. Half-precision vectors: already done, so the item is replaced by dropping one unused index

**Found.**
- Both tables are already `halfvec(1024)`, created so: `mx_embeddings` in `0068:129-137` and `mx_entity_vectors`
  in `0072:114-122`.
- The worker sends float16 (`scripts/markx-worker:375-380`).
- Every cosine bar is already computed on float16.
- halfvec's storage is EXTERNAL (`pg_type.typstorage = 'e'`), so the ~2,056-byte vector goes to TOAST
  uncompressed.

So 2.8 GB of vectors are float16 already. A conversion gains nothing. **Dropped as asked.**

**What the bytes are.**
- The vector in TOAST: ~2.1 kB a row.
- Two 64-character hex hashes, the uuid and the model: ~0.2 kB a row.
- The HNSW index: ~2.7 kB a row. On the `prep_mxreal` clone it is 51 MB for 19k rows.

**The replacement: drop `mx_embeddings_v_hnsw_idx`.**
- Its one reader is `repo.nearest` (`repo.py:444-447`), and only a test calls that (`test_markx_storage.py:252`).
- A post's neighbours are exact cosines over its window, by primary key (`pairing._cosines`, `pairing.py:574-579`;
  `embed.current_candidates`, `embed.py:464`).
- Every other reader scans or aggregates.
- The entity index stays: `identity.vector_neighbours` (`identity.py:1466-1472`) orders by it in full runs.

**Interface change.** `repo.nearest` goes too (the deletion test: it has no caller), and the test's index
assertion changes.

**Migration (0148).** `DROP INDEX CONCURRENTLY` can't run inside a transaction, so this needs the migrator's
non-transactional form, or a plain `DROP INDEX` (it takes milliseconds; the table is locked briefly).

**Gain.**
- About 0.4 GB (148k rows × ~2.7 kB). Measured after the deploy by `table_sizes`.
- Every embed answer's insert stops maintaining an HNSW graph. Measured by the store's seconds on a clone.

**Check before building.** `idx_scan` of the index on the server must be 0. Today only the owner can read it
(`pg_stat_user_indexes`). The slice adds index usage to `GET /api/operations/db-statements` (v1021-db's read-only
route), so it is a GET from then on.

**Not proposed.**
- A smaller dimension: it changes results.
- `STORAGE PLAIN`: it would save only TOAST's chunk overhead, and the gain is not measured.

### 4. Ingestion: the time is after the fetch, and the asked parts are already built

**Found already built.**
- **Conditional GET.** `ingestion/http.py:350-372` `Validators` sends If-None-Match and If-Modified-Since; they
  are kept per address in `AddressMemory` (`:396`) and dropped after 7 days. Kinds with `conditional` (generic RSS,
  page watch) use it (`runner.py:20-25, 469-473`). The server's run events show sources answered "unchanged".
- **A per-source schedule by posting rate.** `ingestion/schedule.py:38-49`: the interval is clamped between 1 h
  and 21 days from the mean gap between posts; there is a 6 h probation interval and backoff of 1 h up to 24 h (7
  days after 10 failures). So 15-28 of 359 sources are due an hour, as the job records show.
- **Bounded parallel fetch.** `runner.py:395` `asyncio.gather` under the global semaphore
  `INSIGHT_FETCH_MAX_CONCURRENCY` = 8 (`gates.py:259`), with one request in flight a host and a learned gap per host
  (`HostLimiter`, `gates.py:38-63`).

**Those three are dropped as asked.**

**The friction measured.** After the fetch, `IngestionRunner.run` (`runner.py:404-413`) waits for:
1. `self.stories`: Mark IX's story job, in a process of its own, inline in the run. During a rebuild it waits for
   the rebuild slice's lock and works the queue.
2. The originals within their budget (`originals_service.py:819-833`), then Mark X's scout and research ticks
   (`markx/scout.py:909-924`).
3. `_keep_derived` (`ports.py:275-293`) and `profiles_after_tick` (`run_scheduler.py:354`).

That is the 132-388 s of every run, two to eight times the fetch.

**The deepened module.** The jobs runner (`markx/jobs.py`, the job "stories", since 0209) becomes the story job's
only driver.
- The ingestion run, having saved posts, only **wants** the story job: `jobs.want(cur, "stories")`, the mark
  `labels.ask` and Discover use. The 0059 triggers have already queued the posts. It no longer runs the job.
- The runner's `due()` picks "stories" when it is wanted or a rebuild is due. `STORIES_GAP` (120 s) still spaces
  the slices, a lock still keeps one at a time, and a wanted run starts within POLL (30 s).
- The originals, scout and research ticks follow the story job in the same child. They read its stories, and
  their order is kept.
- `IngestionRunner`'s `stories` and `originals` ports go away. One owner, and one fewer path that runs the story
  job (`main.py:114-133`'s start run is the same want).
- The run's record says what it fetched and saved. A "Fetch now" (Trigger.MANUAL) still wants the story job, and
  its stories show at most ~30 s plus the job later.

**Expected gain.**
- An hourly run's wall time becomes its fetch: 50-142 s instead of 229-438 s. Measured from the job records'
  `duration_s` after the deploy.
- The new posts' stories land within one look of the save.
- The scheduler's own peak no longer includes the originals' page reads (item 8b).

**Risks.**
- The originals were "by the scheduler's runs only, never a Fetch now in the API". A wanted job is still the
  scheduler's.
- The tests of the runner's ports (`test_ingestion_*`) change to assert the want.

**The fetch's own time.** The X sources through Nitter wait the host's 10 s floor (`HOST_FLOORS`). Ten X sources
take ≥ 100 s, by design (Nitter rate-limits). Not changed. A per-source schedule already keeps quiet X accounts
from being fetched every hour.

### 7. Light imports per process: dropped as asked; the memory is the scheduler's retained heap (item 8b)

**Found.**
- The backend imports no heavy library. `requirements.txt` holds fastapi, pydantic, uvicorn, psycopg,
  cryptography, telethon (lazy) and yt-dlp (a subprocess). Parsing is the standard library's.

### 8. A rule change never regroups the whole archive at this cost

**The modules today** (`services/stories_service.py`, `db/repo_stories.py`, `utils/stamps.py`).
- `rules_stamp()` (`stories_service.py:1256-1267`) is **one** fingerprint of five outputs of the sample: the
  stories, their edges, every post's keys, the name surges and News.
- Any difference starts the same rebuild (`step`, `:282-292`), whatever changed:
  - keys: every post's keys, 2,000 a step;
  - threads: every X source's timeline;
  - names: every title judged and News over the window, then `redo_every_post` (`repo_stories.py:760-765`) queues
    `redo:` for every post;
  - stories: batches of 1,000 (2,000 on v1021-db), each regrouping its neighbourhood (~7x the batch, ~11
    groupings), then News, Classics and Discover of the batch.
- `take_queue` orders by `queued_at, key`, and every `redo:` shares one `queued_at`. So the order is uuid order,
  random by date: the window the pages show first is finished last on average.
- A batch that changes nothing still deletes its keys, upserts the `stories` setting after three `count(*)`
  (`_settle :237`), moves `discover_at` (`:249-252`) so Discover's served answer is rebuilt, and rewrites
  Discover's kept counts (`_discover_step :669`).

**The friction, on the server.**
- The running rebuild is for a **News** rule (0209). News is rebuilt by its names step in seconds, before any
  `redo:`, yet it has regrouped 53,868 posts in 50.6 h and changed 0 stories.
- On v1021-db the batches are ~4.8x faster (~2 h estimated for 158k posts). That is still a full regroup, with the
  container's memory sawtoothing and the DB busy, for a change that touched none of it.

**The deepened module: `stamps` + `StoriesService.step` own "what a rule change must redo".**
1. **Three stamps, one fingerprint each** (`rules_stamp()` returns `RulesStamp(keys, grouping, news)`, each
   fingerprinting its own outputs of the same sample):
   - `keys`: `{p.id: keys(p)}`;
   - `grouping`: `stories, edges`;
   - `news`: `surges, news()`.

   The stored setting keeps the three (`rules` becomes `{keys, grouping, news}`). An old single value reads as
   "all three changed", which is what it means today.
2. **`step` decides the smallest redo, in one place:**

   | what changed | what runs | server cost |
   |---|---|---|

#### 8b. The scheduler's ~1.3 GB

**Verified from the code and v1021-db's measurement.**
- Jobs are started with `multiprocessing` "spawn" (`jobs.py:1088`, `stories_service.py:1293`): fork, then exec.
  Linux carries the fork's resident high-water into the child's `ru_maxrss` across exec. That is why every job
  recorded 1,341-1,367 MB (a parent at 1,213 MB gave a child `ru_maxrss` 1,215 MB with its own VmHWM 16.8 MB).
- **The children do not hold that memory.** Exec gives them a fresh address space. The figure was an accounting
  error, already fixed on v1021-db (`utils/peak.py`: VmHWM, `reset()` between views).
- The **parent itself** really did reach ~1.37 GB, its VmHWM at the forks. Its current RSS is not exposed. The
  container's ~0.9 GB mean says it holds most of it.

**Why it holds it.** The scheduler is a long-lived process that runs heavy work in itself and never gives memory
back. Its heavy work:
- every ingestion tick: 8 fetches in flight, bodies up to 10 MB, the originals' and the profiles' pages;
- archive continuations: sitemaps and Telegram histories (`run_scheduler.py:328-346`, `archive_jobs.py`);
- the start's five repairs, with unbounded `fetchall()`s (`repair_0137.py:62-145`).

It runs `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` (compose) but never trims. `utils/idle_trim.py` is wired only into the API, where
0202 measured 1.2 GB fall to 142 MB once trimmed.

**The deepened module: `utils/idle_trim` serves every long-lived process, not only the API's.**
- Its interface gains `trim_now()` (a GC collection, then `malloc_trim(0)`: a few ms).
- The scheduler calls it after each tick, each archive step and the start's repairs.
- The heartbeat it already writes every 30 s (`/app/logs/runtime/heartbeat-scheduler`) gains `rss_mb` and
  `hwm_mb`. `/api/status` `health.checks.heartbeats` shows them, so the server is checked by a GET.
- The start's repairs run in a spawned process, as the jobs do (`in_own_process`, the story job's helper). They
  are one-off, so their high-water leaves with them.
- Item 4 removes the originals' and scout's work from the tick.

**Expected gain.**
- Measure first: the heartbeat's `rss_mb` on v10.22 before trimming is switched on (`INSIGHT_SCHEDULER_TRIM=off`).
- Then: the scheduler at ~100-200 MB between ticks (0202's API went 220 to 130 MB), and the container's mean from
  ~0.9 GB to ~0.3 GB. Grafana's mean and the heartbeat's `rss_mb` are the measures.

**Risk.** None for the results. Trimming frees nothing that is in use.
