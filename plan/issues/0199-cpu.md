# 0199 — Where the server's CPU goes, and the story job cut (v10.18 slice CPU)

Status: **built** (branch `v1018-cpu` from main = v10.17, 1890456). Not merged, not pushed, not deployed. No migration.

## The problem (the owner's Grafana, server on v10.16.1 → v10.17)

CPU peaks ~200% (two cores) about hourly for ~10 minutes; insight-ingestion mean 31%, insight-db mean 30%. The hourly
ingestion run's job takes ~8-10 minutes for ~40 new posts.

## How it was measured

- **The server**, read-only, a handful of GETs: `/api/status` (recent_jobs: start and end of each run),
  `/api/ingestion/logs` (application log: each fetch's start, the story job's summary, every Mark X stage with its
  seconds; rss log: full-text reads), `/api/sources/health` (each source's kind, cadence tier, last attempt). Nothing
  written. The application log covers 16:08-17:43 UTC on 2026-11-13 (a deploy of v10.17 at 17:19 in the middle: the
  Mark X lines after it are v10.17's).
- **A server-scale copy** (`prep_scale`, main's migrations: 148k posts, 1.06M tasks, 744k post refs, 4,962 stories),
  cloned per experiment; three decision runs on one clone (the first a full one, 578 s) to give it entities.
  Python CPU is `time.process_time()` of the job's process; Postgres time is every statement timed by its call site
  (a psycopg wrapper in the measuring scripts: pg_stat_statements is not preloaded on either Postgres, and the shared
  scratch server was not restarted for it). Scripts and outputs: `plan/research/cpu/` (private).
- **Measured** = a number read off the server's log or timed on the copy; **inferred** = said so where it appears.

## Where the hour goes (server, measured from its log; per hour)

| work | when | wall seconds an hour | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mark X decision runs | ~11 an hour (16:11, 16:17, 16:22, 16:31, 16:37, 16:42, 16:52, 17:08, 17:14, ...) | **~1,500** (123-165 s each) | one after each worker upload, at most every GAP = 5 min |
| Mark X labels queue | after every decision run | **~360** (32-39 s each, peak 1.3 GB) | a second, non-incremental decision reading (labels.population) |
| Mark X planners | check + cite every 5 min, track every 15 | **~300** (check 6-9 s, cite 9-14 s, track 21-31 s), "0 tasks planned" most times | the 0183 gate rarely skips: the worker's answers change mx_tasks every few minutes |
| ingestion run: fetch | hourly, ~2 min | ~130 | 22-24 sources due of 355 (346 deferred by their cadence tier); the time is politeness spacing (one host's sources ~10 s apart) |
| ingestion run: story job | hourly | **72-122** (summary `seconds`: 80.8, 72.4, 122.4) | Mark IX's incremental job over ~40-350 queued posts |
| ingestion run: originals hook | hourly, after the story job | **~390-420** (17:11:49 → 17:18:16; 16:10:53 → 16:17:52) | inferred mostly waiting: see below |
| Mark X candidates | per new vectors | ~15 | 11-12 s per batch of new posts |

The Mark X work is ~2,160 of the ~2,600 busy seconds an hour (wall, split between the job's process and Postgres):
it matches the Grafana means (31% + 30% ≈ 61% of one core ≈ 2,200 s an hour). **The hourly peak** is the ingestion
run's story job and candidates landing on top of a decision run, its labels build and the planners (the decision run
waits for Mark IX's story job, then starts right after it: "a decision run waits for Mark IX" at 16:10:40 and 17:11:40).

## Phase by phase

### Fetching (measured on the copy, per source; the server's run from its log)

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| sources fetched an hour | 22-24 (17:08 run: 24 attempts; the tiers' intervals sum to 21.9 fetches an hour) |
| download of a feed | 0.3-0.5 s (network) |
| parse of a feed | 0.005-0.09 s CPU (10 items: 0.005 s; an 840 kB feed of 1,386 items: 0.09 s) |
| save of a feed's posts, all known (an unchanged feed fetched again) | 0.12-0.45 s CPU, 0.2-0.84 s wall (20-40 upserts) |
| the run's fetch phase, CPU | **~7 s an hour** (inferred: 24 × ~0.3 s) |

- **Cadence per source already exists** (schedule.py tiers: 1 h for the fast sources to 21 days, a 6 h probation, the
  backlog guard): 346 of 355 sources were deferred at 17:42.
- **Conditional GET** (ETag / Last-Modified, issue 0044) is used by generic RSS and page watch: 163 + 28 of the 355.
  Nitter (54), the Telegram bridge (51), Substack (20), Reddit, YouTube and the site adapters ask in full. At ~11 of
  those fetches an hour and ≤0.5 s CPU each, conditional GET or a body hash there would save **under 5 s of CPU an
  hour**: not built (a skip that saves nothing measurable is not worth the risk of missing a post).
- **Full text** is read only for new teasers, and a failed teaser again at most MAX_PAGE_TRIES, RETRY_APART apart
  (issue 0062). A walled site's 403s (16 retried in one run, 2 s apart) cost wall time, not CPU.
- **Page watching**: no source has `watch_changes` on (0 of 355), so no page is re-downloaded or diffed.

The fetch phase is not where the CPU goes. Nothing there was changed, so no post can be missed by this slice.

### The story job (Mark IX; measured, an hourly run replayed on the copy)

The replay: the newest posts of the 15 sources that posted last queued (45 posts) as a fetch run's saves queue them,
the series pass's clock set as the server's (the sources with a post in the last hour: 21), the job run at the copy's
last post time. Before (main) and after (this branch) on two clones of one copy:

| | wall s | Python CPU s | Postgres s |
|---|---|---|---|
| main | 64.9 | 50.6 | ~14.7 |
| this branch | **38.6** | **24.5** | ~14.7 |

Where main's went (cProfile): the **series pass 31.6 s** (21 dirty sources, each made again from all its posts:
`link_words` over the 5,469 posts its regex lets through, 26.5 s: every link's address made, `urljoin` and
`normalize_url`, and the text searched once per link, to keep the 1 in 130 posts with navigation words); **News's
name surges 13.4 s** (every 1-3 word phrase of ~70k titles made, to keep the few that are judged names);
`_neighbourhood` 6.8 s; the two `mark_boilerplate` statements 5.7 s.

The fixes (each module that owns the decision; outputs identical by construction and checked on the copy):

1. **utils/series.py `navigation_links`**: the series pass reads a post's links only when one has navigation words.
   Every link's words come from one pass over the marked text; a post none of whose words is navigation returns at
   once, before any address is made; the others go through `link_words` as before and keep its navigation entries.
   A text holding a marker character of its own goes the long way. Checked: equal to `link_words` filtered by
   `is_navigation` on **all 39,933 posts** the pass reads on the copy (312 with navigation): 37.0 s → 11.7 s CPU.
2. **utils/news_rules.py `phrases_among`**: the incremental judge's name surges make only the phrases that start with
   a word some judged name starts with (a phrase starts with its run's first word, so no other can be judged).
   Checked: equal to `phrases(t) & names` on **all 148,197 titles** of the copy, for 300 names (the hourly job's
   size: 6.7 → 4.4 s CPU) and for all 102,097 names. The whole rebuild (every name) keeps `phrases`: there the
   pruning was slower (6.8 → 7.9 s).

**Every table the job writes is identical** after the replay on the two clones (row count and md5 of every row,
the database's own `now()` defaults left out): stories, story_posts, story_mentions, story_keys (1.7M), post_edges,
news_stories, news_posts, classics, series, series_posts, post_refs (744k, with navigation and boilerplate),
post_links, discover_addresses, and the job's settings minus its seconds.

### The originals hook (after the story job)

~6.5 minutes on the server each hour. Measured on the copy: its planning (settle, linked, collect, entities, works,
references, missed, Classics, suppliers, cited) **10.0 s wall, 8.6 s CPU** (7.1 s of it `plan_linked`); a page read
of the backfill's size (three encyclopedia pages, 280 kB to 1 MB): 0.8-1.3 s download, **0.2-0.7 s CPU** to extract.
Its run reads up to BACKFILL_PER_RUN = 12 pages, PAGE_DELAY_S apart, each under its host's gate, whose learned gap
doubles to MAX_GAP_S after a 429 or a timeout. **Inferred**: the 6.5 minutes are mostly waiting on one host's gap,
not CPU (CPU an hour: ~10 s planning + ~12 × 0.5 s). It holds the ingestion run open (the run's job ends after it),
which is why the run "takes 8-10 minutes". To measure it rather than infer: the summary line (`Originals: {...}`)
now carries `seconds` per step (plan, collect, lookup, backfill, suppliers); read it after the deploy.

### The Mark X decision loop (measured on the server; profiled on the copy)

Per run on the server (v10.16.1 and v10.17 alike): candidates 2-12 s, load 0.2, questions and decisions 14-31,
write 0.3, connections 4-9, graph 3-6, back 21-26, later 1, headlines 0, news 1-7, **entities 57-92** (its budget is
45 s), surges 1-5, perspectives_plan 4-10: 123-165 s; then the labels build 32-39 s. The `changes` counts are the same
from run to run (`join` 1986-1992, `new_pair` 1902-1907, ...): most runs recompute what the last one decided.

On the copy, a run with nothing new at all (no answer, no post between two runs): 99-113 s, labels 35-37 s (peak
3.1 GB); Python CPU 68-72 s of it. A profiled run (152 s): `decide.load` 23.7 s per call, read **twice** (the run,
then labels.population's own non-incremental reading); in it `embed.current_candidates` recomputes the cosine of every
embedding candidate row (452k) each call (8.8 s); the entities stage at the copy's state was a resumed full pass
(the copy's own, not the server's daytime case), so its 52-56 s are not compared here.

**Not changed in this slice**, because none of it can be cut with the same outputs for the same inputs at a smaller
scope than its own slice:

- A decision run's outputs depend on the clock (the story window, News's window, back's and later's 72 hours, the
  briefing hour), so no stamp of its inputs says "this run would change nothing" (0183's rule): every run must run.
- The labels queue is a fresh decision reading at its build time, not a copy of the run's.
- The planners' 0183 gate declares `mx_tasks` whole as an input: any answer of any kind moves it.

decision loop overlapping; the decision loop is ~83% of the hour's busy time and is the lever.

## What would cut the decision loop (owner decisions; not built)

Measured sizes, server: each item's share of the ~2,160 Mark X seconds an hour.

1. **Run less often while only answers stream** (jobs.GAP 5 → 15 min; EVERY stays hourly, a story job still wants a
   run): ~11 → ~4 runs an hour, **~-950 s** decision and **~-230 s** labels an hour. Same decisions for the same
   inputs; News and stories in Shadow lag answers by up to 15 minutes instead of 5.
2. **Build the labels queue on demand** (when the Labels tab is opened and the newest run's queue is not kept),
   or at most every 30 minutes: up to **~-330 s** an hour. The tab says "being built" for ~35 s on the first open
   after a run.
3. **The planners' gate by kind** (tasks.background_inputs): stamp only the mx_tasks rows each planner reads (its own
   kind's) instead of the whole table, so an answer of another kind (track answers are most of the stream) does not
   wake check and cite. Exact by construction; the size depends on the stream's mix (≤ ~200 s an hour).
4. **One decision reading per run**: labels.population reads `decide.load` again without the run's incremental
   state (23.7 s at scale); `decide.load` makes every embedding candidate's cosine each call (8.8 s) though the
   window keeps few of them. Both are exact only with a proof against 0169's full-vs-incremental check; a slice of
   their own.
