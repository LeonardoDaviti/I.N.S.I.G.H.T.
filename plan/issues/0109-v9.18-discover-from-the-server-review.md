## Decided
**Slice A (backend Discover):**
1. **A platform account is tested and followed through its own source kind:** the address the kind reads, e.g. the X kind's feed for `x.com/<user>`. Its result is labelled like any other.
2. **Order:**
   - tested-followable first, then untested by score;
   - unfollowable suggestions get their own section, "Can't be followed as a feed", kept in score order, with their reasons and the stories they started;
   - never below untested ones.
3. **A followed source's other channels.** A suggestion that a followed source's own posts link (its profile links, its about page) and whose name matches that source's name is shown as "Also <followed source>'s" in a folded group, not as a suggestion. Measured on the review's pairs: 21 of 23 right. Matching by name alone is Mark X (0101 with 0090).
4. **Not sources, by address shape** (each shape measured on the server's suggestions before adopting it, with counts in Built):
   - web archives and CDN hosts;
   - share links of chat apps (paths like `/share/`, `/c/`);
   - known publishing platforms split by their owner path or subdomain, the way 0080 splits code and model hubs;
   - document and data hosts;
   - paper hosts by 0106's paper-id shape.

   They go under References or are dropped from suggestions (as 0080's references are).
5. **One card per owner domain:** subdomains and sections of one registrable domain are one card, outside hosts where each subdomain is a different owner (0080's shared-hosting list).
6. **Archive rows** count pages, not stories, leave out pages already stored, and say "the feed stopped on <date>" or "this source watches <section>, not <section>" instead of offering Archive… when that is the case.
7. **A read title equal to the site's own name** (its `og:site_name` or the title shared by its other pages) isn't a title: the line keeps its link words or address.
8. **"Feed found" only when the feed holds at least one of the linked pages,** else "a feed, but not of these pages".
9. **Voices:** one owner named once; the language-code rule for a path segment requires the segment to be the whole first path part and in the language list. A handle isn't a language.

**Slice B (addresses and Follow):**
1. **Tracking queries stripped** (the mail and analytics families), in `url_normalization`. This changes stored normalized addresses, so it's a rule-stamp change: the first start regroups stories. Measure the joins it makes in the real-data check and read each one.
2. **Follow…** on a platform account opens Add source with that platform's template filled in (X, YouTube, Telegram, Reddit), not the address field.

What was built, item by item, and what the measures changed:
1. **Platform accounts** (`discover_reads.kind_source`, `kind_test`): an account key becomes the source its kind reads, as the Add source templates write it (x.com/<user> → the Nitter bridge's `/<user>/rss`, t.me/<channel> → the Telegram bridge, reddit.com/r/<sub> → r/<sub>, youtube.com/@<channel>), kept only when `KindRegistry.resolve` gives a kind other than the generic feed or page watching. The test is one `fetch_latest` of 3 posts: "feed" with posts, "blocked" on 401/403, else "headlines". Each test stores `test: FOLLOW_TEST`; one by older rules is due again wherever its suggestion is listed (the X accounts had sunk out of the first page).
2. **The order** (`discover_rules.order`, `section`): the tested followable first, then those tested that can't be followed (`section: "unfollowable"`, "Can't be followed as a feed" on screen, with their label and stories), then the untested, each by score. The reads also test the first page's count by score wherever listed (`discover_reads.to_read`), so an untested suggestion with more stories isn't stuck behind the tested ones.
3. **Other channels** (`own_names`, `same_owner`, `StoriesService._other_channels`): a source's names are its account's, its site's registrable word (a writer's own label on a host of many writers), its display name's words and those run together, 4+ letters only; a key holds a name when one of its words is it, starts or ends with it. Such a suggestion goes to `also` (`of`, `source_id`), a closed group on screen. Tried first: a source's address words as names, which took `research` and `data` from feed addresses; an address as a name now gives only its site's word. Wrong on the server: a cloud company matched by "Cloud" in another source's name; a forge's blog matched by a third party's feed named after the forge. Debatable, on both: a lab's news source taking its parent company's other sites.
4. **Not sources** (`place_kind`: `archive_address`, `app_address`, `document_address`, `paper_address`; `find_platforms`' marked owners): an archive (another full address in the path, or a file); an app's shared pages (an opaque id right after `/share/`, `/c/`, `/chat/`, `/notebook/`, …); documents and data (a first segment naming a kind of record: `/documents/`, `/docket/`, `/statistics/`, `/sota/`, …), a quiet line of references; paper pages (0106's paper id, or a `/paper/` path). A host whose pages mark their owners (`/@x`, `/~x`, `/x/posts/…`) on 60%+ of its pages, under 2+ owners, is a platform of those owners, and only a marked page names one. Tried and dropped: an opaque id anywhere (it took a newspaper's `/content/<uuid>` articles, dated blog slugs, job boards). On the lab's kept links the marked-owner rule finds 14 more platforms, all read right (5 Mastodon servers, 4 universities' `~user` pages, a newsletter host's `/@` profiles, a blog platform, two social apps); the first real-data run made a newsletter host's `/redirect/` an owner, fixed by "only a marked page names an owner".
5. **One card per owner domain** (`one_card_per_domain`, `federation`, `DiscoverRepository.site_hosts`): a registrable domain's hosts are one card led by its best, `places` its hosts ("· also …" on screen), its voices, articles, stories, classics and opens added up; outside a domain of many owners: a hosting platform (`site_of`), a site your sources publish on at 2+ hosts (`shared_sites`), or 10+ linked hosts at under 4 linking posts a host. That bar is measured on the lab's kept links: a blog host 51 hosts at 2.0 posts a host, a newsletter host 556 at 2.3, three universities 31–47 at 1.6–3.6, a union's 28 at 2.5; one company's 49 at 15.5, 27 at 5.2, 15 at 16. Shared linking voices between hosts were tried first as the sign and told nothing apart (a blog host 17%, a news site 17%, a lab 0%). Wrong on the server: a senator's and a committee's hosts on one government domain (9 hosts, under the bar).
6. **Archive rows** (`_archive_rows`; `archive_states` also returns the stored pages, the newest post, the page-watch prefix): `origins` counts the pages the source still lacks (pages, not stories), `stored` those it has stored since (their stories join at the next story run), and a row with none left goes; `stopped` when the newest post is 6+ months old; `watches` {prefix, not} when a page watch reads another section; `archivable`, the pages an archive can reach. The screen says so, and offers Archive… only when an archive can reach some.
7. **Site-name titles** (`discover_reads.page_title`, `page_titles.shared_titles` and `read`, `read_titles`): a read title equal to the page's `og:site_name` is no title; in the stored reads, a title 2+ pages of one host share is no title (pages told apart by their last segment and query, a tracking query aside: one page at two paths keeps its title), in Discover's lines and Classics. Only reads are judged, as the review asked: two pages truly titled alike (a product launched twice, two job ads) lose only a read title.
8. **A feed of these pages** (`label`, `own_pages`): the finder is given the suggestion's own linked pages (an owner's, under its path); "feed" only when a found feed holds one (or none were linked), else `other_feed` ("A feed, but not of these pages"), listed with those that can't be followed as a feed. FOLLOW_TEST 3: every stored test is made again once.

Dropping the two-letter rule was tried and made a store's `/dp/` and `/gp/` pages owners (283 links): two letters still name no owner. The address rules' stamp now holds platform samples, so these rows refill.
